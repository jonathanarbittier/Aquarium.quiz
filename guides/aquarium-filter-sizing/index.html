How to Size an Aquarium Filter

A manufacturer’s aquarium-capacity range is a useful eligibility check. It is not a calculation of fish waste, aquascape resistance, or the amount of maintenance your tank will need.

Reviewed October 30, 2026

1. Estimate actual water volume

The nameplate volume of a tank is not always the volume of water in operation. Substrate, rocks, wood, an air gap, and internal equipment displace water. Use a realistic estimate when comparing flow and dosing; use the tank’s nominal size as a conservative reference when checking whether equipment physically and operationally fits.

2. Treat the published range as a boundary

Do not recommend a filter below its published maximum capacity. Within a product family, ratings and flow can change sharply. Fluval’s AC line, for example, publishes distinct ranges from 5–20 gallons for the AC20 through 60–110 gallons for the AC110. The range tells you which model the manufacturer intends for a tank size; it does not promise identical performance in every aquarium.

3. Add headroom for the reason you actually need it

Extra capacity may help with large fish, heavier feeding, or a high-waste setup, but “oversize everything” is not a complete rule. A larger filter can also create excessive intake or return flow, occupy more space, and cost more to service. Headroom is useful only when the livestock can tolerate the flow and the owner can maintain the equipment.

4. Separate flow from capacity

Gallons per hour and tank rating describe different things. Flow describes water movement under specified test conditions. Capacity combines the manufacturer’s intended aquarium range with the filter’s design and media. Fluval publishes both pump output and lower filter-circulation figures for FX models, demonstrating why a single headline GPH number should not be substituted for a tank rating.

5. Check coverage and redundancy

Long aquariums can have quiet zones even when total flow looks adequate. Intake and return placement matter. Aqueon notes that aquariums of 100 gallons or more may require multiple filters. Two filters can improve coverage and provide partial filtration during maintenance, but they double the number of intakes, cords, and service points.

A sizing checklist

  • The tank falls inside the manufacturer’s published range.
  • The intake and output are appropriate for the most flow-sensitive animal.
  • The filter fits the rim, cabinet, hoses, and maintenance path.
  • Replacement media, seals, and impellers are available.
  • The maintenance schedule is realistic for the owner.
