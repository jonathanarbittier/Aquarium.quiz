Filtration Planning for Large Aquariums

Large tanks are not simply small tanks with a larger gallon number. Their length, equipment weight, hose runs, service volume, and failure consequences all increase.

Reviewed October 30, 2026

Think in zones

A long aquarium can meet a total flow target while still collecting debris at one end. Map where each intake will draw from and where each return will push water. Rockwork and large decorations can block circulation. Aqueon notes that tanks of 100 gallons or more may need multiple filters because of their length.

Use manufacturer figures carefully

Fluval publishes the FX6 for aquariums up to 400 US gallons, with 563 GPH filter circulation and a 20-liter canister. EHEIM publishes the Classic 1500XL for approximately 300–1500 liters, with an 18-liter filter volume. Those figures help establish eligibility and physical scale, but neither manufacturer can account for your fish size, feeding, rockwork, or maintenance interval.

Redundancy can be more useful than one oversized unit

Two independent filters can improve end-to-end coverage and allow one unit to keep running while the other is serviced. Redundancy is not automatic protection: both filters may still stop during a power outage, and both need separate maintenance. Put critical equipment on appropriately planned circuits and verify restart behavior after power is restored.

Measure the service path

Record cabinet opening width, internal height, hose bend radius, valve access, and the distance to a sink or drain. The EHEIM 1500XL has a large 23-liter container, while the FX6 is roughly 21 inches tall. A specification that fits inside the cabinet may still be impractical if the lid cannot be removed or the filled canister cannot be lifted safely.

Do not convert GPH directly into capacity

Penn-Plax publishes 820 GPH and “150 gallons+” for the Cascade Max-Flow, but its manufacturer catalog does not state the 164–273 gallon rating found on some retailer pages. TankPicks therefore does not rank that model until a manufacturer-confirmed upper capacity is available. Flow alone is not enough to invent one.

Plan maintenance before purchase

  • Choose valves that isolate hoses without draining them.
  • Leave room to remove media baskets vertically.
  • Decide where dirty filter water will be carried or drained.
  • Keep seals, impellers, and critical fittings available.
  • Stagger service on multiple filters rather than cleaning everything together.
