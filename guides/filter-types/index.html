Aquarium filter guide

Aquarium Filter Types Explained

Filter labels describe where equipment sits and how water moves through it. They do not, by themselves, tell you whether a filter suits your livestock, maintenance routine, or available space.

Reviewed October 29, 2026

Start with the job, not the format

Every aquarium needs a way to capture debris and support biological filtration. Chemical media is optional and task-specific. Aqueon’s filtration overview separates these roles into mechanical, biological, and chemical stages; one device may perform all three, but the balance and media volume vary.

FormatUseful strengthsImportant tradeoffs
Hang-on-back (HOB)Easy access, visible return flow, compact media chamberNeeds rim and wall clearance; the return can be strong in shallow tanks
CanisterLarge external media volume, flexible media arrangement, discreet displayRequires cabinet space, hoses, priming, and deliberate leak checks
InternalNo external hoses; useful where external filtration is impracticalOccupies tank space and may be visually prominent
SpongeGentle intake, simple biological surface, useful for fry and shrimpNeeds an air pump and usually provides limited debris polishing
SumpFlexible equipment space and large system volumeRequires an overflow, return pump, plumbing, and careful water-level planning

Hang-on-back filters

A HOB filter pulls water into a chamber and returns it over the aquarium edge. It is easy to inspect and usually faster to service than a canister. Check the tank’s rim thickness, the gap behind the aquarium, the minimum operating water level, and whether the outflow can be reduced. Seachem publishes separate wall, trim, and internal-clearance dimensions for its Tidal models; that is a useful reminder that gallon rating alone does not guarantee physical fit.

Canister filters

A canister sits below or beside the aquarium. Its main advantage is media space outside the display. Its main cost is service complexity: hoses must be routed without kinks, valves and seals need inspection, and a filled canister can be heavy. Confirm cabinet height and door clearance before purchase, not after the filter arrives.

Internal and sponge filters

Internal filters keep the pump and media inside the aquarium. Fluval describes the U3 as a primary filter for smaller tanks or a supplementary filter for larger tanks, with three output positions for different flow patterns. Air-driven sponge filters are even simpler. They can protect small livestock from a powered intake, but the air pump, airline, and check valve are separate parts of the system.

Sumps are systems, not just filters

A sump can hold mechanical media, biological media, heaters, probes, a protein skimmer, and the return pump. It also introduces overflow and return-plumbing decisions. Choose it when you want a configurable system and can maintain stable operating and power-off water levels.

Practical choice: pick the simplest format that provides enough published capacity, safe flow, usable media space, and maintenance access for your actual aquarium.
